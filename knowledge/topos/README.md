# Knowledge Topos prototype content

This is a synthetic teaching prototype for the Group → Group Action → Representation interaction. It was written for the website, independently of the private Obsidian Vault. It is not presented as the user's manuscript, research, proof certification, or complete course. Definitions and examples are independently worded statements of standard mathematics; source books, PDFs, illustrations and lecture prose are not copied into this directory.

`prototype.json` is the data contract: 22 concepts, 40 directed typed relations, 67 Markdown sections, and nine reference sources. `section.markdown` is relative to this directory. `concept.sections` lists that concept's own sections. `section.children` may reference a section owned by another concept when it supplies a prerequisite or further explanation. The section graph is acyclic. The level is semantic detail, not a maximum recursion depth: the group has the chain `group-overview → group-definition → group-associativity → group-example`, with its last two sections both at level 3.

The initial concept is `group`; the other primary transition IDs are `action` and `representation`. Relations have mathematical explanations. `strength` and `lenses` are editorial relevance settings for this prototype, not measured mathematical dependence, proof strength, or semantic-model similarity. Evidence uses `sourceId#source section; section:local-section-id`. These source section labels identify where the mathematical material can be checked; they are not assumed to be website URL fragments. Only relation types justified by these examples are instantiated. The larger relation-type vocabulary does not imply that this small dataset contains an adjunction, duality, contradiction or categorification.

The Markdown protocols `concept:<id>` and `section:<id>` ask the client to focus a concept or unfold a section in the current field. Standard HTTPS source links remain external references. No page-routing objects or layout positions are encoded here.

The content checks protect several conditions that would otherwise be easy to lose in a short explanation: quotient **groups** require a normal subgroup; the orbit–stabilizer correspondence uses a coset **set**, whose stabilizer need not be normal; group-algebra elements have finite support; the module equivalence uses unital left `k[G]`-modules; characters here are restricted to finite-dimensional complex representations of finite groups; irreducibility excludes zero and depends on the chosen field. Maschke's theorem is stated only for finite groups over the complex numbers. Its full proof is outside this prototype.
