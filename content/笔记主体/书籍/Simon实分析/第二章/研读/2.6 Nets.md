> English mathematical text; Chinese reading remarks. This is maintained working material, not a record of personal mastery.

The metric-space tests from Chapter 1 can fail in general topology: convergent sequences may fail to determine closed sets, continuity, the topology itself, or Hausdorffness. The cocountable topology supplies several failures at once. The repair is to index convergence by the collection of tests that actually defines the topology.

## Replace the time line with directed refinement

A directed set lets any two completed tests be refined together. A net is a function from such a set into the space; convergence still means eventual membership in every neighborhood. A cluster point requires frequent membership, a weaker condition. Simon's subnet definition uses an eventually cofinal reindexing map, without requiring that map to be monotone. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Nets and General Convergence|The net page]] fixes these conventions and keeps the two quantifier patterns separate.

If $x\in\overline A$, direct all neighborhoods of $x$ by reverse inclusion and choose a point of $A$ in each neighborhood. This constructs a net converging to $x$ without any countable-base hypothesis. Closed-set inverse images then prove that preservation of all convergent nets is equivalent to continuity. Directing pairs of neighborhoods shows that two points with no disjoint neighborhoods are simultaneous limits of a single net; thus uniqueness of all net limits characterizes Hausdorffness.

## Compactness becomes a convergence theorem again

A cluster point determines a convergent subnet by jointly refining a tail index and a neighborhood. Compactness forces every net to have a cluster point: otherwise a finite cover of eventually avoided neighborhoods produces an impossible common tail. Conversely, a cover with no finite subcover indexes a net of points avoiding each finite subfamily; that net cannot have a cluster point. These complete proofs are maintained together in the knowledge page.

The weak-* compact dual-ball example in the source illustrates failure of subsequence compactness but uses Banach–Alaoglu from Chapter 5. It is explicitly a forward-reference example, not a premise needed to establish the net theorems. Lower semicontinuity and metric completeness have corresponding net tests, whose detailed exercise tasks are separately indexed.
