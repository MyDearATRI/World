A topology may have too many local tests for sequences, or too few continuous functions to distinguish points. This section adds countability and separation hypotheses and then combines them to represent the space inside a countable cube.

## Countability controls how tests can be arranged

[[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Bases and Countability Conditions|The countability page]] distinguishes first countable, second countable, separable, and Lindelöf. In a metric space, second countability, separability and Lindelöfness are equivalent; first countability is automatic and strictly weaker. The proofs turn countable dense centers into rational-radius balls, or countable covers at scales $1/n$ into a dense set.

For first countable spaces, nested neighborhoods let one choose a sequence converging to any closure point and a subsequence converging to any cluster point of a sequence. The same construction restores the continuity criterion for maps with first countable domain. This identifies the extra structure silently available in the metric arguments of Chapter 1.

## Separation produces real functions

Simon includes $T_1$ in both regularity ($T_3$) and normality ($T_4$). [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Separation and Continuous Extension|Urysohn's lemma]] constructs a continuous $[0,1]$-valued function separating two disjoint closed sets in a normal space. Its proof first builds dyadically indexed open sets with strict closure nesting; inverse images of intervals then verify continuity. The distinction between open nesting and closure nesting is a genuine proof issue.

Tietze extension uses repeated Urysohn approximations of a bounded continuous function on a closed subset. Its statement and geometric-error strategy are recorded, while the complete exercise solution remains in the source task. Exact zero sets require closed $G_\delta$ sets: a uniformly convergent sum of separating functions vanishes precisely on the designated set. Exactness becomes essential in the next construction.

## Coordinates reconstruct the topology

For a second countable normal space, every closed set is a $G_\delta$. Choose functions $f_n$ positive exactly on each basic open set $U_n$. The map $F(x)=(f_n(x))$ into the Hilbert cube is continuous and injective. More than injectivity is required: $F[U_n]=F[X]\cap\{u_n>0\}$ shows that the inverse on the image is continuous. Pulling the cube metric back gives a compatible metric. The complete argument is in [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Metrization and the Hilbert Cube|metrization and the Hilbert cube]].
