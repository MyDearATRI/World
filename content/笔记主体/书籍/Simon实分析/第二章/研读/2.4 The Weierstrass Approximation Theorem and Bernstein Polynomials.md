Uniform approximation must control the worst error over an entire interval. Bernstein's formula achieves this with a weighted average of sampled function values. Its reliability comes from nonnegative normalized weights that concentrate near the evaluation point.

## Construct the approximation before passing to a limit

For $f\in C([0,1])$ and an integer $n\ge1$, put $B_nf(x)=\sum_{j=0}^nf(j/n)\binom njx^j(1-x)^{n-j}$. The constant and first-moment identities give $B_n1=1$ and $B_nx=x$; the second centered moment is $x(1-x)/n$. For a threshold $\delta$, split the error into samples within $\delta$ of $x$ and samples farther away. Uniform continuity controls the first part, and the second moment bounds the total far-away weight. The resulting estimate is
$$
\|B_nf-f\|_\infty\le\omega_f(\delta)+\frac{\|f\|_\infty}{2n\delta^2}.
$$
The complete proof is maintained in [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Positive Approximation and Bernstein Polynomials|positive approximation and Bernstein polynomials]]. It uses finite binomial sums and does not presuppose the laws of large numbers in Chapter 7. An affine change of variables yields polynomial density on every nondegenerate compact interval; a singleton requires only a constant polynomial.

## What density says, and what it leaves open

The first Weierstrass theorem concerns ordinary polynomials on an interval. The second concerns trigonometric polynomials on the circle, allowing both $e^{in\theta}$ and $e^{-in\theta}$. Restricting to nonnegative powers gives an obstruction: uniform limits preserve the vanishing of certain Fourier integrals, while $e^{-i\theta}$ violates that vanishing. This concrete obstruction motivates the conjugation hypothesis in §2.5.

The source also introduces Korovkin sets. A positive linear approximation process can sometimes be checked on finitely many test functions; on $[0,1]$ the tests $1,x,x^2$ control the same concentration information as the Bernstein moments. The knowledge page states the theorem and the mechanism. Its full proof, multivariate extensions and rates remain in the indexed source exercises rather than being silently filled in.

## Preparation for later approximation methods

[[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Algebras, Lattices, and Uniform Approximation|Stone–Weierstrass]] will use only polynomial approximation of $|t|$, converting it into a lattice operation. Chapter 3 will return to explicit averaging in the Fejér kernel. These two continuations have different outputs: the structural theorem guarantees existence of good approximants, while a positive averaging construction proves convergence of one specific process.
