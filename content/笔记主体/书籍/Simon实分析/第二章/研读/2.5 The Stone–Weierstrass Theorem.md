Instead of inventing a new approximation formula for each compact space, ask whether the available functions already have enough algebraic structure. Constants and point separation permit exact interpolation at two points. Multiplication and uniform closure then create the maxima and minima needed to patch those local interpolants.

## The lattice is the intermediate object

Let $X$ be compact Hausdorff and let $S\subset C(X,\mathbb R)$ be a subalgebra containing constants and separating points. In its uniform closure, approximate $|f|$ by polynomials in $f$. The identities for $f\vee g$ and $f\wedge g$ then turn that closure into a vector lattice. This is the one place where the preceding Bernstein/Weierstrass result enters the structural proof.

[[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Algebras, Lattices, and Uniform Approximation|The algebra and lattice page]] maintains the full argument. Its second stage fixes one point, interpolates at that point and each other point, and takes a finite minimum to obtain a global upper error bound without losing equality at the fixed point. A second finite cover and a finite maximum produce the lower error bound. Compactness is therefore used in a precise pair of local-to-global steps.

## The extra condition for complex functions

A complex subalgebra must also be invariant under conjugation. This places the real and imaginary parts of its functions back inside the algebra, so the real theorem can be applied. Polynomials in $z$ alone on the circle show that point separation and constants do not replace this hypothesis. Trigonometric polynomials satisfy it and are uniformly dense, supplying a later route to completeness of the Fourier system.

The source ends with two useful variants: functions vanishing at a designated point of a compact space, and functions vanishing at infinity on a locally compact Hausdorff space. The first follows by adding and then subtracting constants; the second uses [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Local Compactness and Compactification|one-point compactification]]. A nonvanishing condition at each original point is needed to separate it from infinity.
