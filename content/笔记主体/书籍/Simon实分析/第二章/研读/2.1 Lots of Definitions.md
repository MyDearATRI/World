The central question is which parts of metric analysis depend on distance and which depend only on neighborhoods. A topology keeps the open sets and their union/intersection rules. It therefore retains closure and continuity, while quantitative estimates such as a common modulus of continuity still require a metric on the relevant space.

## From open sets to continuous maps

Begin with [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Open Sets, Closure, and Continuity|the topology and continuity page]]. The relative topology explains why a set can be open or closed only within a specified ambient set. Closure is characterized by every neighborhood meeting the set; accumulation additionally removes the point itself. These distinctions are useful immediately when passing from sequences to topology: a limit must lie in the closure of its terms, but a closure point need not be obtainable from a sequence in an arbitrary space.

Continuity is defined by inverse images of open sets. It implies preservation of convergent sequences, but the reverse statement from Chapter 1 is deliberately withheld until countability hypotheses are examined. The initial topology generated by a family of maps gives a way to construct a topology by deciding which numerical or coordinate observations should be continuous. This will later produce both products and weak topologies.

## The quantitative layer that remains

For continuous functions into a metric space, uniform convergence preserves continuity. The proof chooses one approximating function uniformly close to the limit, then uses that function's continuity and a three-term triangle inequality. If the codomain is complete, a uniformly Cauchy sequence of bounded continuous functions has a pointwise limit and the same uniform estimate promotes convergence to uniform convergence. These complete proofs are maintained on the topology page. The pointwise limit of $x^n$ on $[0,1]$ is the counterexample that keeps the quantifiers visible.

[[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Semicontinuity, Extrema, and Continuous Partitions|Semicontinuity]] retains only one side of continuity. A lower semicontinuous function has closed sublevel sets, and arbitrary suprema of continuous functions are lower semicontinuous. This prepares an existence argument for minima rather than just adding another definition.

## A global consequence: connectedness

The final movement of the section concerns [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Connectedness and Components|connectedness and components]]. Connectedness rules out a separation by two nonempty open sets; path connectedness constructs continuous curves. Connectedness survives continuous images and closure, whereas the oscillating-graph example prevents reversing the path implication. Local path connectedness supplies the missing local condition and proves that open connected subsets of $\mathbb R^n$ are path connected.
