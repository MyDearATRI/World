> English mathematical text; Chinese reading remarks. This is maintained working material, not a record of personal mastery.

Compactness makes local information usable globally by allowing a finite subcover. The section explores several consequences of that one principle: no loss of subsequential limits in metric spaces, attainment of extrema, compactness of function families, and finite continuous patching.

## The metric and topological descriptions

[[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Compactness and Total Boundedness|Compactness and total boundedness]] maintains the complete equivalence between metric compactness, sequential compactness, and completeness plus total boundedness. Total boundedness supplies nested finite-radius selections; completeness puts their Cauchy limit inside the space. Compact Hausdorff spaces are normal, compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed, and continuous bijections from compact to Hausdorff spaces are homeomorphisms. These conclusions depend on different hypotheses and should not be blended into one unspecified compactness rule.

The source calls a space weakly sequentially compact when every sequence has a cluster point. For general topological spaces this does not mean that every sequence has a convergent subsequence. The agreement in this section's metric equivalence comes from first countability, while §2.6 supplies the general net replacement.

## Extrema and families of functions

A nonempty compact space supports a minimum for a lower semicontinuous extended-real function bounded away from $-\infty$; the sublevel sets near the infimum are nested, nonempty and closed. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Semicontinuity, Extrema, and Continuous Partitions|The extrema and partition page]] gives the proof and separates the identically $+\infty$ case. It also constructs continuous partitions subordinate to an open cover of a compact set in a normal ambient space, and proves increasing continuous approximation of lower semicontinuous functions on a compact metric space. The alternative infimum-regularization proof is explicitly a note supplement.

[[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Equicontinuity and Compact Families|Arzelà–Ascoli]] combines compact values, diagonal subsequence selection on a countable dense set, equicontinuity, and then compactness of the domain. The full foundational proof is expanded in the knowledge page, although Simon assigns it to Problem 1. Compactness of the domain is what converts pointwise convergence into uniform convergence.

## Local compactness and the added point

[[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Local Compactness and Compactification|One-point compactification]] adds a point whose neighborhoods are complements of compact subsets. Local compactness and Hausdorffness ensure that this point can be separated from each original point. Functions vanishing at infinity become continuous functions on the compactification that vanish at its new point, preparing the locally compact Stone–Weierstrass theorem.

The source also relates metrizability of compact spaces to separability of their continuous-function spaces. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第二章/知识/Metrization and the Hilbert Cube|The metrization page]] records the conditions and proof route rather than duplicating another long coordinate construction.
