## Source corrections and open checks

The following points were compared with rendered images of the local AMS 2015 PDF. “Correction” identifies a local mathematical or typographical issue in that copy, not an assertion that a published errata list has been consulted. Source text remains unchanged.

| Location | Checked issue | Local treatment and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| p. 116, (3.1.38)（来源 PDF，第 137 页；原文件未公开） | The displayed upper bound uses $\Vert x\Vert ^2$, although the vector being expanded is $y$. | Read $\sum_\alpha\vert \langle x_\alpha,y\rangle\vert ^2\le\Vert y\Vert ^2$. The finite Bessel inequality and the immediately following (3.1.40) give the same bound. |
| p. 120, Theorem 3.2.1（来源 PDF，第 141 页；原文件未公开） | The printed statement says closed convex set without explicitly excluding the empty set. | State nonempty closed convex set. The choice of a minimizing sequence and existence of a minimizer require nonemptiness. This makes an implicit hypothesis explicit. |
| p. 126, §3.3, Problem 2（来源 PDF，第 147 页；原文件未公开） | The target $Y$ is not explicitly required to be complete. | Use a Banach target, or another explicit hypothesis ensuring the proposed limits lie in $Y$. Without it, the identity from finite-support sequences into the same incomplete space would be forced to extend to all of $\ell^2$, which is impossible while retaining the identity on a dense set. |
| p. 126, §3.3, Problem 4（来源 PDF，第 147 页；原文件未公开） | Conditions (i) and (iii) omit nonzero-function and distinct-point qualifications. | Read (i) for $f\ne0$ and (iii) for $x\ne y$. The zero function and identical points otherwise contradict the conditions immediately. These are explicit logical qualifications, not a new RKHS theorem. |
| p. 134, Theorem 3.4.5（来源 PDF，第 155 页；原文件未公开） | The listed finite-dimensional models start at $n=1$. | Include $\mathbb C^0=\{0\}$ if zero Hilbert spaces are allowed. The nonzero classification and proof are unaffected. |
| p. 139, remark after Hölder continuity（来源 PDF，第 160 页；原文件未公开） | Differentiability alone on a compact manifold is stated as sufficient for Lipschitz continuity. | Use a bounded derivative, in particular $C^1$ regularity on a compact interval or compact manifold with a fixed smooth metric. A localized $x^2\sin(1/x^2)$ example is differentiable at zero but has unbounded derivative nearby and is not Lipschitz. |
| p. 152, Theorem 3.5.18（来源 PDF，第 173 页；原文件未公开） | The final clause refers to $x\in(0,1)$ although the statement concerns $e^{i\theta}$ on the circle. | State convergence at every angle to the average of one-sided limits. | p. 174, (3.7.2)（来源 PDF，第 195 页；原文件未公开） | The printed right side is $\langle A^*\psi,\varphi\rangle$. | The identity consistent with (3.7.1) is $\langle\varphi,A\psi\rangle=\langle A^*\varphi,\psi\rangle$. Testing $A=I$, $\varphi=1$, $\psi=i$ already distinguishes the two formulas. |

For simple tensors the proposed criterion is false: a factor can be identically zero while the other is non-Cauchy. |
