#用途/正式 #类型/研读 #主题/实分析

Barry Simon, *Real Analysis*, AMS, 2015 · §3.3 · pp. 122–130 · PDF 143（来源 PDF，第 143 页；原文件未公开）–151（来源 PDF，第 151 页；原文件未公开）.

The dual space records all continuous scalar-valued linear measurements. The section first explains continuity for linear maps in general normed spaces, then identifies the dual in the special geometry of a Hilbert space. This order prevents the representation theorem from appearing to apply to every algebraic functional.

## What continuity buys

For a linear map, continuity at zero gives a global bound by scaling a small ball. Conversely that bound controls differences and gives continuity everywhere. The operator norm measures the best bound. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Linear Functionals as Vectors#Boundedness and continuous extension|The bounded-map argument]] also explains extension from a dense subspace into a complete target and why the dual of a normed space is Banach even when the original space is incomplete.

The book next proves that a nonzero linear functional on a topological vector space has either a closed kernel, in the continuous case, or a dense kernel, in the discontinuous case. The general proof uses nets; the normed-space version has a direct small-vector construction, recorded in [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Linear Functionals as Vectors#Kernels and discontinuous functionals|the kernel discussion]]. The distinction matters because the next argument needs a closed hyperplane, not just a codimension-one algebraic subspace.

## Recovering the representing vector

For $L\ne0$ continuous, choose a nonzero $\eta$ orthogonal to $\ker L$. Every vector is the sum of a kernel vector and a multiple of $\eta$. Therefore matching the value of $L$ on $\eta$ determines a representing vector on all of $H$. The calculation is
$$
L(x)=\langle v,x\rangle,\qquad
v=\overline{L(\eta)}\eta/\|\eta\|^2.
$$
[[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Linear Functionals as Vectors#Riesz representation|The full proof]] handles the zero functional, uniqueness and equality of norms. It uses §3.2, with no assumption that an orthonormal basis has already been constructed. This is the principal proof dependency of the section.

Because the first inner-product slot is antilinear, $v\mapsto L_v$ is anti-unitary. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Linear Functionals as Vectors#^insight-ch3-riesz|The conjugation remark]] makes the convention explicit rather than calling this a complex-linear identification. The same convention determines the adjoint formula in §3.7.

## Reproducing kernels as a controlled extension

The exercise block introduces spaces of functions with bounded point evaluations. Such an evaluation is represented by a vector $k_t$, giving $f(t)=\langle k_t,f\rangle$. This application is meaningful only after the bound is checked. Ordinary $L^2$ evaluation fails that test, whereas specially chosen function spaces may satisfy it.
