#用途/正式 #类型/研读 #主题/实分析

Barry Simon, *Real Analysis*, AMS, 2015 · §3.1 · pp. 109–119 · PDF 130（来源 PDF，第 130 页；原文件未公开）–140（来源 PDF，第 140 页；原文件未公开）.

The chapter begins by extending the finite-dimensional geometry of Chapter 1 to vector spaces where infinite limits matter. Its first task is to justify that an inner product really produces a metric. The section therefore postpones completeness until after the inequalities: antilinearity and sesquilinearity determine the algebra, positivity produces a length, and the triangle inequality makes that length into a norm.

## Follow the exact identity before the inequalities

For a finite orthonormal family, subtract the component detected by its coefficients. The remainder is orthogonal, so the squared norm is exactly the sum of the squared coefficients and the squared remainder. The progression is
$$
\text{Pythagorean identity}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{Bessel}\
\Longrightarrow\ \text{Cauchy–Schwarz}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{triangle inequality}.
$$
The full derivations and equality condition for Schwarz are maintained in [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Hilbert Completeness and Norm Identities#The geometric identities|the norm-identity page]]. Keeping the residual term until the final step explains what equality means; no inequality needs to be memorized as an unrelated estimate. The parallelogram law has a different later role: it controls distances between competing near-minimizers in §3.2.

The Jordan–von Neumann theorem reverses part of the construction: a norm is induced by an inner product precisely when the parallelogram law holds. Polarization tells us what the candidate inner product must be. Verifying its sesquilinearity is the remaining task assigned in Problem 4, not already achieved merely by writing that candidate formula.

## Separate completeness from separability

Simon defines a Hilbert space to be complete and separable, with the inner product linear in its second argument. Completeness gives limits of Cauchy sequences. Separability later gives a countable basis and permits diagonal extraction. The metric completion and extension of the inner product are checked in [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Hilbert Completeness and Norm Identities#Completion preserves the inner product|the completion proof]]. The examples $\mathbb C^n$ and $\ell^2$ make both requirements concrete. The Bergman-space example is a preview: its completeness depends on complex analysis outside this chapter's established prerequisites.

The completion of continuous functions in the integral square norm supplies the chapter's $L^2$. It is deliberately available before measure theory, so its elements are currently completion classes. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Hilbert Completeness and Norm Identities#^insight-ch3-evaluation|Unbounded point evaluation]] explains why pointwise statements cannot be read directly from an arbitrary completion vector.

## The passage to infinitely many components

The final theorems extend Bessel and Pythagoras by taking finite-subset sums. Square summability makes the tails small; completeness produces their limit. Maximality is not yet assumed, so a residual component may remain. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Orthonormal Expansions#Expansion and Parseval|§3.4]] will remove precisely that residual. This separates the existence of an orthogonal sum from the claim that it reconstructs every vector.
