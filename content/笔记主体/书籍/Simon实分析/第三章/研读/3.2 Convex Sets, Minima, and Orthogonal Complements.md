#用途/正式 #类型/研读 #主题/实分析

Barry Simon, *Real Analysis*, AMS, 2015 · §3.2 · pp. 119–122 · PDF 140（来源 PDF，第 140 页；原文件未公开）–143（来源 PDF，第 143 页；原文件未公开）.

The question now is whether a point has a best approximation in a prescribed set. In finite dimensions compactness often supplies a minimum after restricting to a bounded region. Infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces require another argument, because their norm unit balls are not compact. The parallelogram identity supplies exactly the missing control.

## Distance minimization produces its own convergent sequence

Let $C$ be nonempty, closed and convex. Choose points whose squared distances to $x$ approach the infimum. Convexity makes every midpoint admissible. The parallelogram identity then bounds the distance between two near-minimizers by their excess squared errors. Thus the minimizing sequence is Cauchy; completeness gives a limit, closedness keeps it admissible, and continuity gives the minimum. The same inequality proves uniqueness. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Nearest Points in Convex Sets#Proof — Near minimizers must approach one another|The full proof]] keeps all four roles visible, including the necessary nonemptiness assumption implicit in the book.

This is more information than an existence argument alone: any sufficiently good near-minimizers must lie near one another. The mechanism is retained in [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Nearest Points in Convex Sets#^insight-ch3-minimization|the discovery about Cauchy control]]. It does not invoke the weak compactness theorem that comes much later in this chapter, so the proof has no circular dependence on orthogonal bases or Riesz representation.

## From a convex set to a linear subspace

For a general convex set, the closest point obeys a one-sided variational inequality. For a linear subspace, arbitrary real and imaginary directions are admissible, so that inequality becomes orthogonality. The result is $H=M\oplus M^\perp$ for a closed subspace $M$. Uniqueness makes the map $x\mapsto P_Mx$ linear; Pythagoras makes it contractive. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Nearest Points in Convex Sets#Orthogonal decomposition and its projection|The maintained proof]] also shows that an orthogonal projection has closed range.

This sharpens [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第一章/知识/投影、直和与不变子空间#投影与两部分直和一一对应|Chapter 1's algebraic decomposition]]: the complement is now specified by the inner product and the projection is automatically continuous. Closedness cannot be omitted. A dense proper subspace has zero orthogonal complement, so it cannot form the asserted direct sum with that complement.
