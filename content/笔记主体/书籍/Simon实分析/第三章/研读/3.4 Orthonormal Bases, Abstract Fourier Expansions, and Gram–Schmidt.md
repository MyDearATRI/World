Barry Simon, *Real Analysis*, AMS, 2015 · §3.4 · pp. 131–136 · PDF 152（来源 PDF，第 152 页；原文件未公开）–157（来源 PDF，第 157 页；原文件未公开）.

The preceding inequalities guarantee that square-summable orthogonal sums exist. This section asks when those sums reconstruct every vector. The answer is maximality: if a nonzero residual remained, normalizing it would enlarge the orthonormal family.

## Completeness of a family and completeness of the space

An orthonormal basis is a maximal orthonormal family. It has dense finite linear span, but its expansion of a general vector is an infinite norm limit. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Orthonormal Expansions#Expansion and Parseval|The equivalence proof]] distinguishes the two uses of completeness: the space's metric completeness constructs the sum, and the family's maximality makes the residual zero. Parseval is then the exact equality of vector norm and coefficient norm.

This theorem also gives the best approximation in each finite span. The partial sum has the same coefficients as the target in the retained directions, leaving an orthogonal residual. Its squared error is the sum of the omitted squared coefficients. This is the bridge from [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Nearest Points in Convex Sets|nearest-point geometry]] to numerical truncation and classical Fourier series.

## Constructing the family

In Simon's separable setting, a countable dense set is available. Discard linearly dependent vectors, then subtract successive projections and normalize. Independence prevents division by zero, and the finite spans remain unchanged, so density survives the process. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Orthonormal Expansions#Constructing a basis with Gram–Schmidt|The construction]] provides a complete proof rather than appealing to maximality alone. If separability is removed, the analogous existence argument uses Zorn, as indexed in Problem 1.

Triangular change-of-basis matrices express preservation of the successive finite spans. The related Gram determinant and Cholesky factorization form the exercise branch. Orthogonal polynomials give a concrete example: apply Gram–Schmidt to $1,x,x^2,\ldots$ in an integral inner product. Density of the original polynomial family is a separate condition when an orthogonal family is to be called a basis; Chapter 2's approximation theorem supplies it on a compact interval with the square-integral norm.

## Classification and its boundary

Taking coefficients identifies a nonzero separable Hilbert space unitarily with $\mathbb C^n$ or $\ell^2$. The zero space is included separately. This classifies abstract Hilbert spaces by Hilbert dimension, but it does not identify specified operators, point evaluations, or a tensor factorization. Nor does a countable orthonormal basis become a countable Hamel basis: [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Orthonormal Expansions#An orthonormal basis is not a Hamel basis|the vector with coefficients $1/n$]] requires an infinite sum.
