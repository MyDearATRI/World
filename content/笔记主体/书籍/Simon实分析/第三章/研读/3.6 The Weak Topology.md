#用途/正式 #类型/研读 #主题/实分析

Barry Simon, *Real Analysis*, AMS, 2015 · §3.6 · pp. 168–174 · PDF 189（来源 PDF，第 189 页；原文件未公开）–195（来源 PDF，第 195 页；原文件未公开）.

The chapter returns from concrete Fourier series to Hilbert-space topology. The new goal is compactness. A closed norm ball is too large to be compact in infinite dimensions, but restricting what a neighborhood tests changes that conclusion. The weak topology tests finitely many scalar pairings at a time.

## The topology is defined by all continuous functionals

Riesz representation makes each functional an inner product with one vector. Weak convergence of a net therefore means convergence against every fixed test vector. An orthonormal sequence converges weakly to zero but keeps norm one; this first example demonstrates the topology's difference from the norm topology. If the norms also converge to the norm of the weak limit, the inner-product expansion of the squared error forces norm convergence. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Weak Topologies and Dual Pairings#Definitions and the first separation of convergence modes|Both facts]] are proved locally.

## Why bounded balls admit countable tests

A countable orthonormal basis gives a metric for coordinatewise convergence. On a ball, arbitrary test vectors can be approximated by finite basis sums, and a uniform norm bound controls the residual tail. Thus coordinate, metric and weak convergence agree on that ball. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Weak Topologies and Dual Pairings#Coordinate convergence on a bounded ball|The estimate]] is the step that uses boundedness; coordinate convergence on the whole space is insufficient.

Now take a sequence in the ball. Successive subsequences make successive coordinates converge, and the Chapter 1 diagonal method preserves every fixed coordinate limit. Finite Bessel estimates pass to the limit, proving the limiting coordinates remain square-summable with the same bound. The bounded-coordinate theorem gives a weakly convergent subsequence. Since the restricted topology is metrizable, sequential compactness becomes compactness. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Weak Topologies and Dual Pairings#Weak compactness of a ball|The full weak compactness proof]] visibly uses all three chapters: diagonal extraction, metric compactness, and orthogonal expansion.

## The global topology retains a different size

For infinite-dimensional $H$, the weak topology on all of $H$ has no countable neighborhood base. Finitely many tests leave an infinite-dimensional orthogonal direction untested; a supposed countable base would force a countable algebraic spanning family, which cannot span a complete infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. The detailed contradiction is maintained in [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Weak Topologies and Dual Pairings#Why the whole weak space is not metrizable|the nonmetrizability proof]]. There is no conflict with metrizability of individual bounded balls.

The source goes further with a net that is eventually orthogonal to every fixed finite set while its norm tends to infinity. This distinguishes nets from weakly convergent sequences, whose boundedness is a later uniform-boundedness result. Finally it introduces strong and weak operator topologies, now maintained with their examples in [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Operators, Adjoints, and Operator Topologies#Three topologies on operators|the operator page]].
