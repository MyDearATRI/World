#用途/正式 #类型/研读 #主题/实分析

Barry Simon, *Real Analysis*, AMS, 2015 · §3.7 · pp. 174–176 · PDF 195（来源 PDF，第 195 页；原文件未公开）–197（来源 PDF，第 197 页；原文件未公开）.

This section introduces operator classes for the later operator-theory volume. Its local mathematical construction is the adjoint. The other definitions describe structures already visible in the preceding sections and indicate which finite-dimensional intuitions require modification.

## Move an operator through a pairing

For fixed $x$, $y\mapsto\langle x,Ay\rangle$ is bounded and linear, so Riesz gives a unique vector called $A^*x$. Consequently
$$
\langle x,Ay\rangle=\langle A^*x,y\rangle.
$$
The variable order is part of the definition, not typography. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Operators, Adjoints, and Operator Topologies#The adjoint from Riesz representation|The construction]] checks linearity, boundedness, $(A^*)^*=A$, equal norms, and the $C^*$ identity $\|A^*A\|=\|A\|^2$. It also records the correction to the printed (3.7.2) with a link to the page image's location.

## Distinguish the classes

An isometry preserves every vector norm; a unitary also has full range. The unilateral shift shows why that extra condition matters in infinite dimensions. A partial isometry preserves norm on its initial subspace and vanishes on its orthogonal complement, with $U^*U$ and $UU^*$ its initial and final projections. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Operators, Adjoints, and Operator Topologies#Isometries, unitaries, and projections|The operator page]] connects this to the orthogonal projections already constructed in §3.2.

Self-adjointness, positivity and normality are distinct definitions. Positivity implies self-adjointness, while self-adjoint and unitary operators are both normal. Compactness refers to norm-compact closure of the image of the unit ball. It does not mean simply that the operator is bounded. Finite-rank operators and the infinite-dimensional identity illustrate the difference.

## What is being advertised rather than proved

The polar decomposition and compact self-adjoint spectral theorem are previews from Part 4. The multiplication operator $f(x)\mapsto xf(x)$ is bounded and self-adjoint but has no eigenvectors, showing why an arbitrary infinite-dimensional self-adjoint operator does not inherit [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第一章/知识/内积、自伴算子与有限维谱定理|the finite-dimensional eigenbasis conclusion]]. The source's eigenvector argument explicitly waits for the measurable-function realization of $L^2$; this dependency is retained rather than silently used before Chapter 4.
