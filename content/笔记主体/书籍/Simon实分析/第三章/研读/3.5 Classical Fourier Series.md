#用途/正式 #类型/研读 #主题/实分析

Barry Simon, *Real Analysis*, AMS, 2015 · §3.5 · pp. 137–168 · PDF 158（来源 PDF，第 158 页；原文件未公开）–189（来源 PDF，第 189 页；原文件未公开）.

This long section turns the abstract expansion theorem into several different results about periodic functions. Its organizing question is not simply whether a Fourier series converges, but what kind of convergence is claimed and which feature of the function or summation operator provides it. The source develops density, pointwise and uniform convergence, rough continuous examples, and behavior near jumps, with additional directions in its historical notes and exercises.

## First establish the Hilbert expansion

With $e_k(\theta)=e^{ik\theta}$ and normalized measure $d\theta/(2\pi)$, orthogonality is immediate. Completeness of this family is not immediate. The first proof uses complex Stone–Weierstrass: finite trigonometric sums form a unital self-conjugate point-separating algebra on the compact circle. Uniform density then implies $L^2$ density, and the construction of $L^2$ supplies the final completion step. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Trigonometric Density and L2 Fourier Expansion#From density to norm-convergent Fourier series|The complete chain]] gives $S_Nf\to f$ in $L^2$ and Parseval.

No pointwise conclusion is hidden in that norm limit. Moreover uniform density asserts that appropriate polynomials exist; it does not identify them with the Fourier projections. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Trigonometric Density and L2 Fourier Expansion#^insight-ch3-density|This distinction]] is central to reading the rest of the section without conflating its theorems.

## Two kernel arguments with different hypotheses

For the ordinary partial sum, the Dirichlet formula gives
$$
S_Nf(\theta)=\int D_N(t)f(\theta-t)\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}.
$$
The kernel changes sign. Near $t=0$, its envelope behaves like $1/|t|$, so a Dini condition controls the local error. Away from zero, oscillation makes the Fourier coefficients vanish. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Dirichlet Kernels and Dini Convergence|The Dini page]] expands both the pointwise proof and the compact-family argument needed for uniform convergence under a uniform Dini modulus. It also explains localization: changes far from a point do not affect an existing local limit.

Cesàro averages use the positive Fejér kernel instead. Nonnegativity, integral one and concentration give a uniform bound followed by uniform approximation for every continuous function. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Fourier Series and Summation Kernels#Positive approximate identities|The approximate-identity proof]] identifies each hypothesis's job. On the noncompact domain $\mathbb R^d$, bounded uniform continuity replaces the automatic compact-circle property, while a common compact kernel support permits local uniform convergence for continuous functions.

## Use frequencies to construct counterexamples

The lacunary family $\sum a^nn^\gamma\cos(b^nx)$ converges uniformly in its stated parameter range. To prove it rough, the source isolates a coefficient using a Fejér kernel lying inside a large frequency gap. A hypothetical Hölder or Lipschitz condition forces that coefficient to decay too fast. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Lacunary Fourier Series and Roughness|The full gap estimate]] proves the stated nowhere-regularity ranges and marks the borderline Jackson-kernel case as a further exercise. It does not infer nondifferentiability merely from divergence of a formal derivative series.

For a step, ordinary partial sums develop nonvanishing overshoot in a region of width comparable to $N^{-1}$. [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第三章/知识/Pointwise Fourier Convergence and Jumps#The step and the Gibbs scale|The rescaled integral profile]] shows both the limiting amplitude and the moving locations of the extrema. Positive Fejér means stay within the step's range, yet still cannot converge uniformly to a discontinuous function. Fixed-point convergence, supremum error and integrated squared error measure different behavior.

## Keep later results in their actual role

Jordan's theorem, stated in the historical discussion, gives the half-sum of one-sided limits for functions of bounded variation. Its proof is deferred to the exercise using §4.15. The historical discussion also mentions the much deeper almost-everywhere Fourier convergence theorem for $L^2$; that theorem is cited by the book and is not proved in this chapter. It must not be used here as if it followed from Parseval.

The discrete Fourier transform in the notes on pp. 154–155 replaces integration by a sum over roots of unity. With the book's normalization it is $\widehat f(m)=N^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f(j)e^{-2\pi imj/N}$ and inversion has no $N^{-1}$ factor. The FFT's even/odd split reduces computation for powers of two to order $N\log N$. This is an algorithmic introduction, separate from the analytic convergence theorems; no full implementation or numerical-error analysis is claimed here.
