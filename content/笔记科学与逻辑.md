#用途/导航 #类型/路线 #主题/知识组织

> 笔记科学与逻辑 · 给读者与作者的说明 · 英文说明完整用法，中文帮助快速定位。

> 原始材料保留出处；笔记主体写完整的阅读、推导与思考；知识主干再从实际正文提炼可复用内容。新文章先进入主体，新书和新领域有自己的主体位置。

The work begins with a continuous mathematical account in the notes, or 笔记主体. Read a book, follow a course, examine an article, or develop an independent argument; preserve enough explanation for another reader to follow what the objects are, why the questions arise, and how the reasoning develops. Reusable concepts, results, methods, and connections can then be distilled from that actual text into the knowledge backbone, or 知识主干.

The order matters: first write the account, then decide what deserves an independent reusable entry. A book's table of contents or a collection of unwritten topic names remains that book's writing plan. It does not become a knowledge backbone through a change of folder name. As more books and fields are added, continuous notes retain their context while the backbone grows around the mathematics they share.

A reader should be able to find what a claim means, why it is available, and where its limits lie. An author should be able to preserve an unfinished thought without presenting it as an established result. This guide explains how the parts of the vault support those two tasks. Its working rules are editorial conventions; the limited research evidence discussed below is identified separately.

## 2. The parts of the system

> 主体保存完整思路，主干保存提炼后的可复用知识，原材料保存出处。写作规划、标签与图谱帮助定位，不替代正文。

For sustained reading, enter the [[笔记主体/笔记主体|notes]] and choose the account you want to follow. For a precise reusable result, consult the [[知识主干|backbone]] when such an entry has actually been developed. For a claim about a source, return to its original page. These are different reading tasks, so their entrances remain distinct.

The eventual dictionary is an index into genuinely developed backbone entries. The Simon topic index is a local book index; it has been named accordingly. Likewise, a book-specific unit may remain useful for organizing that book even when the wider backbone adopts a different division of the subject.

Continuous reading does not require every argument to reside in one physical file. Linked sections or embedded proofs can form a coherent account. What matters is that the reader encounters the required assumptions, mathematical steps, and transitions without having to reconstruct an argument from disconnected fragments.

## 3. How to read a mathematical page

> 沿着当前论证读；遇到真正缺少的前提再跳转。先看对象与条件，再看策略，最后检查具体推理。

Start by identifying the task of the page. What is being defined, constructed, proved, compared, or left open? Read the short Chinese note for orientation, then read the English statement with its full assumptions. Check the underlying space, scalar field, domain and codomain, quantifiers, and the sense of convergence or regularity involved. A familiar name can conceal a different convention.

On the first pass through a long proof, identify its goal and the sequence of intermediate tasks. On the second pass, inspect the step that makes each transition possible. Ask where a hypothesis is used, whether a map is well defined, and whether the argument has proved existence, uniqueness, or both. For a short proof, one careful continuous reading may be sufficient; the page does not need to be forced into a fixed number of passes.

Open a link when it supplies something the current argument genuinely needs: a definition you cannot recall, a cited theorem whose hypotheses matter, or a counterexample that determines the scope of a conclusion. Leave comparison links until the main argument is clear unless the comparison is the subject of the page. Returning to the original argument is part of reading the link.

A concrete starting point is [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第一章/知识/Cauchy 序列与度量完备化|Cauchy sequences and completion]]. First locate the definition and the existence-and-uniqueness statement. Then follow the construction through the sections on candidate distances, density, completeness, and compatible uniqueness. The link to [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第一章/知识/等价关系与商构造|equivalence and quotient constructions]] supplies a construction used in the argument. The warning about real-number completeness explains why the general construction cannot simply be used to justify its own prerequisite. These links have different logical jobs even though both are displayed in the same way.

After reading a substantive result, try one small action without looking at the page: recover its assumptions, explain the difficult step, or work through a relevant example. Reopen the note and compare. Record the specific gap you find, if any. Being able to recognize a proof on the screen and being able to reconstruct or apply it are different observations; neither should be silently converted into a claim of mastery.

If the page is only an outline, stop expecting an argument from it. Its useful next actions are to open a source, consult an existing related note, or begin a Working draft. Empty structure does not need to be read as if it contained completed mathematics.

### Reading and maintaining insights

The [[笔记主体/发现归档|Insights and Additions archive]] lets you revisit a useful consequence, an assumption exposed by a proof, or an intuition without first reopening every chapter. Its passages are embedded directly from the notes. Read the English claim with its conditions, use the Chinese remark for orientation, and check the stated source identity and proof status. The link below each excerpt returns to the discussion that justifies it.

An archive entry is an invitation to inspect an argument. A structural observation may explain a standard theorem already stated in the source; a supplementary consequence may strengthen what the source explicitly says in that location. A heuristic helps organize a proof but cannot replace one. These identities refer to the text and its evidence, not to originality or to the reader's mastery. Source errata and unresolved exercises remain in their chapter's exercise and source-check page.

For example, the completion excerpt explains why uniqueness is relative to a fixed embedding. The source theorem already includes that compatibility; the note supplies the omitted argument. The finite partition excerpt identifies a consequence of the constructed functions: their sum is one on an open neighbourhood of the compact set. The two excerpts have different source relationships even though both may be new to the reader.

> 先看发现本身，再看它的身份与条件；需要使用这个结论时，回到原证明。归档帮助回看，不替你判定已经理解或掌握。

When writing, first finish the relevant passage in its continuous context. If a short observation is worth returning to, place an `insight` callout near that argument, give it a stable block identifier, and embed that block in the archive. Keep the long proof in the note. Revise the original passage when its condition, conclusion, attribution, or proof status changes; the embedded text follows the revision. If the file moves, repair the link while retaining the block identifier. A repeated submission updates the existing entry rather than creating another copy.

The archive is a way to read the body. Deciding that a result deserves an independently maintained backbone entry is a separate editorial decision governed by [[笔记科学与逻辑#5. Writing first, then distilling|the distillation criteria]]. New books and fields retain their own body entrances and may contribute excerpts to this same archive.

## 4. The anatomy of an entry

> 栏目是不同的阅读任务，不是必须填满的表格。定义、直觉、证明和疑问应当容易辨认。

A page earns its place by treating something that can be discussed and reused with its conditions intact. Its size follows that task. Keep a definition with the examples needed to understand it; keep the essential argument near the result it establishes. Split off a proof, example, or method when several pages need to cite it independently and the extracted page remains intelligible.

The opening motivation identifies a real problem: an existence question, a distinction between two constructions, an estimate, or an obstruction. The definition fixes the object precisely. A prototype example shows the definition at work; a non-example or counterexample identifies a boundary. Equivalent formulations provide additional ways to recognize or use the object, but each equivalence needs the conditions under which both directions hold. A structural interpretation explains what the construction organizes or preserves without replacing its definition.

The result states what can now be concluded. Its proof strategy identifies the main idea; the proof supplies the argument. Dependencies name the particular results being used. Connections explain a comparison, application, or limitation. Further questions preserve actual remaining work. Sources make the account traceable, and local revision notes record changes that affect how it should be understood. Include these parts where they help this page; omit empty rituals.

The intended object labels have stable meanings. Theorem, proposition, lemma, and corollary all require sound justification; their names distinguish editorial roles rather than degrees of truth.

| Label | Meaning | What the reader should expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEF | Definition | Objects, notation, and exact defining conditions. |
| THM | Theorem | A central result with explicit hypotheses and a declared proof status. |
| PROP | Proposition | A result serving the local development. |
| LEM | Lemma | An intermediate result used in a larger argument. |
| COR | Corollary | A consequence whose dependence on an earlier result is made explicit. |
| EX | Example | A particular object or calculation illustrating the current statement. |
| C.EX | Counterexample | A precise failure of a proposed statement or weakened hypothesis. |
| RMK | Remark | A supplementary observation or interpretive qualification. |
| IDEA | Key idea | Intuition or strategy, with its relationship to the formal argument explained. |
| ? | Question | An unresolved task; any attempts or partial results retain their status. |
| ! | Warning | A condition, notation issue, or failure that could invalidate an inference. |

Earlier pages often use Chinese callout titles instead of these abbreviations. Read their actual statements and labels; the visual migration has not yet been applied to the whole vault. Neither a box nor a `THM` heading tells you whether the proof is expanded here, cited from elsewhere, or still incomplete. That information belongs beside the claim.

### A proof as a readable path

For a complex proof, give the reader a goal and a strategy before the details. Use intermediate headings that state the work being done, such as “The definition is independent of representatives” or “The constructed space is complete.” A compact skeleton can show the route, but the text must justify each step. Name the difficult transition and expose the hypothesis that supports it. Routine checks can be concise when the verification is actually available to the intended reader.

Do not divide every short argument into “Step 1” through “Step 6.” A paragraph can carry a complete short proof. Conversely, do not describe an unfinished plan as a proof merely because its sequence of headings looks persuasive. Mark the exact missing implication or construction.

### English exposition and Chinese reading notes

The main English text must be sufficient to understand and check the mathematics. Chinese notes provide quick orientation: why this step matters, which hypothesis to watch, or how a source's notation differs. They need not translate every sentence. When an essential assumption first appears in a Chinese note, move it into the English statement as well. The same condition should not be available only to bilingual readers.

## 5. Writing first, then distilling

> 先写主体，再提炼主干。提炼后的内容回链主体；主体可以引用或嵌入主干中的稳定证明，保持连续可读。

Start with the material and the question you have. If you are reading a book chapter, write a chapter account. If you are working through an article or your own calculation, preserve its course of thought. Clarify objects and assumptions, expand important steps, and keep unresolved parts visible. You do not have to fill an abstract concept inventory before doing this work.

After an actual account exists, ask whether some part can usefully stand on its own. A candidate may be a definition with its distinguishing examples, a theorem with conditions and proof, a reusable method, or a precise comparison. Inspect the existing backbone before creating it. One source can be enough: cross-source organization means that the entry is defined by its mathematics and can accept other sources later, not that two books must already have been read.

Distillation involves editing for independent use. Replace source-specific transitions with a clear local problem, expose prerequisites and conventions, check the argument or declare its cited proof status, and retain a link to the original discussion. Do not extract a sentence if its missing conditions remain elsewhere. Do not extract a whole chapter merely to reproduce the book's outline under a new name.

Give a reusable long proof one maintained home. Once it has been distilled, the body note can keep its motivation, local statement, assumptions, examples, and transitions while embedding or precisely citing the maintained proof. This preserves the original reading experience and avoids competing copies. A source link from the backbone records provenance; a link from the body records reuse. The two directions have different jobs.

The diagram shows the order of work and subsequent reuse. Its arrows describe editorial actions, not mathematical implications.

### Stages describe maturity, not location

Scratch, Working, and Canonical describe the state of a piece of writing. Both body notes and backbone entries can have an editorial stage. These values are neither three required directories nor synonyms for “source,” “notes,” and “backbone.” Moving a file does not promote it.

| Stage | What belongs here | What makes a revision worthwhile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scratch | Raw notes, captures, calculations, questions, and failed starts. | Preserve enough context to recover the thought and its source. |
| Working | Organized exposition or an argument with known gaps or unsettled claims. | Clarify assumptions, develop a proof, resolve a discrepancy, or identify what remains. |
| Canonical | An account maintained as a stable reference, with precise conditions, provenance, and proof status. | Correct an error, incorporate a useful new argument, or improve the account without losing traceability. |

Canonical does not require every background theorem to be proved from first principles in this vault. A checked result can cite a precise source for its proof, provided that the distinction from a locally developed proof is visible. An unresolved claim cannot become an established premise by being placed near stable content.

### What the properties mean

Purpose, function, maturity, and mastery are separate. `用途/正式` places a page in the actual knowledge project; `用途/示例` marks a demonstration; `用途/规划` marks writing preparation. `type` identifies a knowledge, reading, connection, question, or route page. `status` describes its editorial condition. `layer` records Scratch, Working, or Canonical where the classification has been assigned.

An older page without `layer` has no recorded stage. Do not infer Canonical from `status: 已整理`, from a book knowledge-page title, or from a folder named “知识主干.” A navigation index does not require a content-maturity label. `aliases` stores useful names, `created` records a date, and `cssclasses` selects presentation. None of these fields records the owner's understanding.

For an unwritten Simon position, the first substantive addition normally remains Working. Update the visible short note to say what exists and what remains; remove `结构/待写` when it is no longer just a placeholder. When the page becomes an actual part of the book's exposition, give it a suitable body location, update its purpose, and repair incoming links. That is a transition from planning to writing, not an automatic extraction into the backbone or promotion to Canonical.

## 6. Links, tags, and diagrams

> 链接表示可以跳转，逻辑关系必须由文字说明。共享标签不等于共同前提，反向链接不等于逆命题。

A link is useful when its surrounding sentence identifies why the target matters. “The construction uses the quotient metric defined here” carries more information than a list of unrelated-looking titles. Link to the relevant heading when only one result is needed. Repeated terminology does not require repeated links, and a page does not need a prescribed number of connections.

Several kinds of relationship must remain distinct. A dependency supplies a premise actually used in an argument. An application checks the hypotheses of a general result in a particular setting. A comparison places two constructions side by side under stated conditions. A counterexample defeats a specific claim. A tentative analogy proposes something to investigate. A source link identifies provenance. Writing which of these is intended prevents a navigation graph from silently becoming a false proof graph.

For instance, the [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第一章/知识/投影、直和与不变子空间#投影与两部分直和一一对应|algebraic account of projections]] and the [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/第一章/联系/从代数投影到正交谱分解|comparison with orthogonal spectral decomposition]] do not license dropping the extra inner-product and self-adjoint conditions. The connection page exists precisely to examine that transition. Its backlink is evidence that a page is mentioned, not that every conclusion works in reverse.

Tags collect pages along reusable dimensions. The vault uses purpose and type tags, topic tags, and, in the Simon writing plan, `domain/...`, `method/...`, and `object/...` tags. A domain gives a local thematic home; a method such as `method/completeness` can be shared across homes. A shared tag says that the pages are worth retrieving together. It does not assert equivalence, prerequisite order, or the presence of the same theorem.

The [[maps/Simon - Planning Map-107623f4|Simon planning map]] is a spatial index for that book. Its groups show writing units, and its labeled lines select reading connections. It is not the map of a completed cross-source backbone. The existing [[maps/第一章关系图-408f620b|chapter-one map]] and [[maps/模板示范关系图-ec20d5e6|template map]] have their own legends. Read each line according to that map's declared meaning. Tags do not automatically draw Canvas edges.

A mathematical diagram has a stronger obligation than an overview map. If a diagram claims commutativity, specify the objects and maps and establish the equality of the relevant composites. If it depicts an exact sequence, state what exactness means in the setting. A diagram can make reasoning visible only when its geometry represents mathematical assertions the text can defend.

The native graph currently filters for `tag:#用途/正式 -tag:#结构/待写` and displays tag nodes. The [[笔记主体/书籍/Simon实分析/写作规划/Simon - Writing Plan#Graph views|graph-view notes]] give alternate filters for the Simon plan, unwritten positions, and local topic groups. Use these views for orientation; dense connectivity is not a measure of understanding or of a page's importance.

## 7. Bringing new material into the system

> 文章先作为完整文档进入主体，随后才提炼。新书、新课、新领域各自容纳实际正文；它们不必塞入 Simon 的单元。

When a document arrives, preserve the original and its available context. Identify source statements, the owner's own attempts, and later assisted additions. Unknown authors, dates, or page numbers remain unknown. A submitted file does not make all of its mathematics an original discovery by the person submitting it.

Choose its body destination by the nature of the work. A new book belongs alongside Simon in the books area; a course belongs with that course; an article belongs in the article area; independent calculations belong with the question being investigated. A thematic lecture belongs in the body even when it uses several books. A document can therefore enter the system before any suitable backbone entry exists.

For a future article intake, the archived source can be kept under `原始材料/文章/`, while its continuous note belongs under `笔记主体/文章/`. Those directories are created when the material arrives. An already written note can be used as the basis of that body page after its original is preserved; it need not be rebuilt as separate topic cards. Existing sources such as the Simon PDF can remain in their current locations.

If the submitted document is already a coherent note, preserve its argument and useful wording while making the necessary local edits. If it is a source article, keep that original unchanged and write the associated reading account. If it is raw course material, first organize it into a readable course note. English exposition and Chinese reading notes apply to the edited account; the original retains its original language.

Only then inspect reusable material. Match actual definitions and conditions against the backbone, not just titles. An additional proof, example, qualification, or notation comparison may improve an existing entry. Repetition may only add provenance. A new object or unresolved issue may need a new substantive Working entry. Preserve the continuous body in every case; extraction is not a demand to turn an entire article into disconnected cards.

For example, suppose a future article note develops a completion construction and an unanswered uniqueness question. This is a hypothetical intake example. The complete article discussion first receives its own body page with the source attached. Compare the construction with the existing Simon discussion. If there is a useful independent account, distill it with the necessary assumptions and links to both discussions; leave the unanswered uniqueness question visibly unresolved. The article remains readable in its own sequence.

As a new field appears, establish a body entrance for its actual material and add a backbone grouping when real entries make that grouping useful. A shared method may connect fields without identifying their objects. Terminological overlap alone does not justify merging results with different conditions.

After writing or extraction, update the affected body entrance, local plan, genuine backbone index, and meaningful connections. Preserve valid filenames and headings, or repair their incoming links when relocation is necessary. Check uses of a changed statement. Update a Canvas only when its represented relation has changed; no background watcher is required.

Append an actual update-log（未公开） entry identifying the input, the body destination, any distilled or revised backbone entries, the checks made, and the remaining work. Describe the change without copying the whole document or inventing learning history. Repeated input with no content change is recorded as checked. The person providing the material does not have to manually move an article through several folders or decide all of its eventual conceptual destinations first.

## 8. Why these reading and design choices are used

> 研究依据、数学要求和本库的设计选择分开说明。检索与解释可以作为阅读动作；它们不会自动证明已经掌握数学。

Some reading actions have relevant empirical support. In two experiments using prose passages, Roediger and Karpicke compared restudy with free-recall testing. Restudy had an advantage on an immediate test, while previous testing supported better retention on delayed tests. This supports including an attempt to recall material, followed here by checking the note. The experiments did not establish that recalling a theorem proves an ability to construct or apply its proof. [Roediger and Karpicke, 2006, *Test-Enhanced Learning*](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x/).

Chi and colleagues examined students' self-explanations while studying worked mechanics examples. More successful learners connected solution actions with principles and conditions and monitored gaps in their understanding. This is a relevant reason to ask why a proof step is allowed and which assumptions it uses. That study does not validate every self-explanation prompt or demonstrate that a particular note layout causes mathematical understanding. [Chi et al., 1989, *Self-Explanations: How Students Study and Use Examples in Learning to Solve Problems*](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1).

The precise application to this vault is an editorial choice: make it easy to find the statement, attempt a reconstruction, check an explanation, and preserve an unresolved question. The English-and-Chinese division, the three stages, the ten Simon writing units, and the chosen typography are not presented as an experimentally validated package. Revise them if actual reading reveals that they obscure the mathematics or make writing unnecessarily difficult.

Mathematical correctness imposes a different kind of constraint. A map must be well defined, an implication must follow, and a quoted theorem must be used within its hypotheses. These requirements cannot be replaced by evidence that a study technique improves memory. For physics, the same discipline also requires separating the model, empirical inputs, approximation regime, and interpretation of the quantities. A formally similar equation alone does not establish that two physical models have the same meaning.

### Visual structure and its limits

The intended style is Mathematical Neo-Modernism: position, whitespace, scale, weight, and then color. A heading locates a logical task; a margin note supplies secondary context; a formula or diagram marks an object the argument needs to inspect. English exposition uses a serif direction, while labels and navigation use sans-serif typography. The exact rendering follows the fonts available on the device.

Use restrained black and gray text with blue for references and conceptual navigation. Repeated roles should have repeated forms, so the reader does not have to decode a fresh design on each page. Leave room around an important formula or theorem, and keep ordinary exposition quiet. Most paragraphs need no box. A long proof should be divided by meaningful stages rather than surrounded by a large decorative container.

Current chapter-one and template pages retain the earlier four-color callouts, while the new navigation and planning pages use the quieter style. Titles and wording remain necessary in both styles; color alone must not identify a mathematical role. A side note may move below the main text on a narrow screen, but the logical order must remain readable. These are criteria for maintenance, not a claim that every older page has already been migrated.

## 9. When to stop, publish, or reopen a note

> 写到足以保存当前工作，就可以停。进入稳定主干前，检查读者能否辨认对象、条件、论证状态与出处。

A Scratch session can end with a recoverable idea. A Working session can end with a precisely identified obstacle. Neither has failed because the typography is unfinished. Preparing a Canonical entry requires a stronger review: can a reader with the stated prerequisites identify the object and hypotheses, follow or locate the justification, distinguish intuition from a formal claim, and verify the source? Do its links explain actual relationships? Are exceptions and unfinished parts visible where they matter?

Stable exposition still permits revision. When an error affects a statement, correct the statement and inspect its uses; record what changed. When a new argument merely offers another route to the same result, explain the difference without implying that the former route was invalid. When evidence is insufficient to resolve a conflict, preserve the competing conditions and reopen the issue in Working.

The author's next action should follow the mathematical state of the work. A missing estimate calls for a calculation; an unclear definition calls for a closer comparison; a confirmed result may need a readable proof; a finished local account may need a connection to the surrounding lecture. Additional formatting is useful when it helps one of those tasks become visible.
